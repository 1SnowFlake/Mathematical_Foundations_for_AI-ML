Section 3 🤖 Robotics Kinematics

Span & Independence: Degrees of Freedom & Singularities

In robotics, actuators provide directions of motion. If two joint directions v₁ and v₂ are linearly independent, their span covers the entire 2D workbench — the end-effector possesses 2 Degrees of Freedom (2 DOF). But if they become parallel (collinear), the cross product drops to zero: the robot enters a kinematic singularity (gimbal lock) and can only move along a single line!

Section 5 🎮 Computer Graphics Engine

Matrix Transformations: The 2D Geometry Engine

In video games, every model, character sprite, and camera is defined by vertices. A 2×2 matrix M = [ col₁ | col₂ ] is the fundamental engine that transforms this space: Column 1 is where î lands, and Column 2 is where ĵ lands. Linearity ensures that straight lines remain straight, parallel lines remain parallel, and the origin (0,0) stays locked in place.

The Master Bridge: How Linear Transformations Power Advanced AI & Engineering

Notice how linear transformations unify seemingly disparate fields. Instead of memorizing isolated formulas, recognize them as spatial manipulation:

🎮

Computer Graphics

Every 3D mesh is rotated, scaled, and projected onto screen pixels via 4×4 homogeneous transformation matrices running parallel on GPUs.

p′ = M_proj · M_view · M_model · p
🤖

Robotics & Kinematics

Forward kinematics chains transformations from base to gripper. The Jacobian matrix maps joint velocities to spatial tool velocities.

T_world = T_base · T_arm · T_gripper
👁️

Computer Vision

Homography matrices match planar scenes between cameras. Camera calibration matrices map 3D optical rays into digital sensor pixels.

x_pixel = K · [R | t] · X_world
📊

PCA & SVD (Data Science)

Principal Component Analysis rotates your dataset onto a new orthogonal basis aligned with directions of maximum variance.

M = U · Σ · Vᵀ (Rotation • Scale • Rotation)
